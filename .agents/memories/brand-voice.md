# Brand voice and writing style

**Read when:** writing or editing any words that appear on the site (the Brand page voice section, the About box, tiles, project descriptions, gallery copy, alt text, the Contact page), or proofreading the user's drafts.

**Standing rule (user): the user writes the first draft, the agent proofreads.** The exception is when the user asks for a draft, as they did for the voice section on 2026-10-13. Then write it, and treat every line as theirs to change.

## The voice, approved by the user 2026-10-14 ("remove ... from the page, then approve")

Three traits, all kept by the user:

| Trait | In practice | Example from the live site |
|---|---|---|
| Plainspoken, clear and down to earth | Say what it is first, then one detail. Short sentences, everyday words, a verb over an abstract noun. | "A 30-slide Instagram series on why some communities struggle to trust doctors." |
| Warm and listening | Listen first. Invite once, speak to the reader directly, say what happens next. | "Tell me what you're working on and I'll get back to you as soon as I can." |
| Dry, with a little wonder (quietly playful) | A small smile and some curiosity, one wink a page at most. Heavy subjects get a calm, caring voice. | "Brewing coffee and conversation alongside my pursuit of my true passions." |

Tone by place: About is friendly and personal. Project pages are clear and calm. Heavy topics like A History of Mistrust are grounded, careful and kind, with no wink. Contact is welcoming and brief. Gallery titles are bold and simple. Alt text is plain and exact.

Words that fit: systems, reusable, built to last, character, mood, craft, clean, clear, accessible, listening, the brief; everyday verbs like use, build, make, listen, reach out.

Writing habits (as published on the Brand page): first person singular with contractions; commas and colons for pauses (no em dashes in prose); at most one exclamation mark a page. The user removed "use US spelling" and "use one title: Brand & Visual Designer" from the page on 2026-10-14; they are not published rules.

## How the user wants this written (their corrections, 2026-10-13)

1. **Few "I" statements.** Write in the style of published brand guides (Mailchimp, Monzo, Atlassian, Slack): a short trait name, then a few plain sentences in the imperative or about the brand, then a real example. Quoted site lines may contain "I"; the guide's own sentences should not.
2. **No "this, not this" comparisons.** No "X, not Y", no "rather than", no "instead of".
3. **Say what the brand is, never what it is not.** No lists of words to skip, no "no wink", no "skip em dashes". Every line states what the voice is or does.
4. **Plain English, little jargon.** Describe tone with everyday, vibe-based adjectives (friendly, calm, curious, cozy, playful, steady, kind, bold), not copywriting terms (imperative, matter-of-fact, register, cadence, dial, mechanics).
5. **Do not draw on the longer bio** (`about_me.md`) for this section. Use lines that are live on the site.
6. No emojis, no em dashes in prose, no underscore emphasis.
